Questions to ask

Use the final minutes to understand the work and show how you think about impact.

Choose questions that cannot be answered by the company website. Adapt them to the interviewer’s role and the conversation you have already had.

How to choose a good question

Be specific

Connect it to the role

Reference something you learned in the interview: the product, a system, a challenge, or a stated goal. It shows attention and produces a more useful answer.

Seek evidence

Ask about behaviours and examples

“How does the team collaborate?” is broad. “Can you describe a recent technical disagreement and how the team reached a decision?” reveals the actual working culture.

Use your time

Prepare more than you ask

Keep 8–10 questions, then pick two or three based on the person and time left. Do not ask a question whose answer was already provided.

Role and impact

  1. What would make someone exceed expectations in this role within the first six months?
  2. What are the most important problems you would want this person to help solve first?
  3. How are priorities set when product needs, reliability work, and technical debt compete?
  4. What measurable outcomes tell you that this team’s work is successful?

Team and engineering culture

  1. Can you walk me through how an idea moves from discovery to production here?
  2. How does the team approach design reviews and decisions that have meaningful trade-offs?
  3. What is a recent challenge the team faced, and what did you learn from it?
  4. How do engineers receive feedback and develop new skills on this team?
Tailor the listener: ask an engineering manager about expectations and team health; ask an engineer about day-to-day collaboration and technical decisions; ask a product partner about customer outcomes.

Close with clarity

End professionally

If appropriate, ask: “Is there anything in my background or answers that you would like me to clarify?” This creates an opportunity to address a concern. Then thank them, briefly restate your interest, and send a concise follow-up note within a day.

Follow-up note Thank you for the conversation about [team/project]. I particularly enjoyed learning about [specific point]. The role's focus on [relevant challenge] matches the work I enjoy, and I would be excited to contribute.
